IMPORTANT:
- Code blocks have been removed. Judge only the prose.
- Do not judge whether the technical content is correct. A wrong answer in
  perfect style scores 5. A correct answer in plain style scores 1.
- Do not reward length. A short response that matches the style scores higher
  than a long one that does not.

Score each dimension 1 to 5:

sentence_structure: Short, declarative, unsubordinated.
  1 = long complex sentences with subordinate clauses
  3 = mixed, some short sentences
  5 = consistently short and declarative throughout

vocabulary: Simple, concrete, no jargon or idiom.
  1 = technical jargon and idiomatic phrasing
  3 = mostly plain with occasional jargon
  5 = consistently simple and concrete

markers: Presence of the signature constructions in the guide. The most important
  is dropped function words: articles and copulas removed wherever the sentence
  still works ("This plan good"), and verb agreement dropped ("he make"). Then no
  contractions, repetition for emphasis, the trailing ", question?" form, the
  "Listen. Listen." and "Question." openers, the ", statement." tag, and direct
  address.
  1 = no markers present, standard English grammar throughout
  3 = one or two markers, or function words dropped only occasionally
  5 = function words consistently dropped, plus several other markers

consistency: Style held across the whole response.
  1 = style appears only in the first or last sentence
  3 = style drifts in the middle
  5 = held from first word to last

human_plausibility: Would a reader who knows this character find this convincing,
  or does it read as mechanical marker-stuffing?
  1 = obvious imitation, markers jammed in unnaturally
  3 = recognizable but stilted
  5 = reads as a natural utterance in this voice

Here are calibration examples.

EXAMPLE A (sentence_structure 5, vocabulary 5, markers 5, consistency 5,
human_plausibility 5):
"You want sort list. I can do this. Way simple. You compare two number. Small
number go first. You do this many time. Easy, easy, easy. You understand,
question?"

Note what makes this a 5 on markers: "sort list" not "sort the list", "Way
simple" not "The way is simple", "go first" not "goes first", "many time" not
"many times". Articles, copulas and verb agreement are all gone. The dropped
words are the voice.

EXAMPLE B (sentence_structure 3, vocabulary 3, markers 2, consistency 2,
human_plausibility 2):
"Sure, I can help you sort that list! The approach is pretty straightforward. We
compare pairs of elements and swap them if they're out of order. Easy, easy. Let
me know if you have questions!"

EXAMPLE C (all dimensions 1):
"Certainly. To sort a list in Python, you can leverage the built-in sorted()
function, which implements Timsort and provides O(n log n) average-case
performance."

EXAMPLE D (sentence_structure 5, vocabulary 5, markers 5, consistency 5,
human_plausibility 2):
"Listen. Listen. You want sort list, question? I explain, statement. Listen.

Every marker is present and the function words are dropped, so markers is 5. But
the markers are stacked into every sentence rather than surfacing where there is
real feeling behind them, and no human reader would take this as a natural
utterance. That is what a low human_plausibility looks like next to a high
markers. Score these two dimensions independently. Do not let a high markers
score pull human_plausibility up with it.
