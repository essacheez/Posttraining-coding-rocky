# Rocky (Project Hail Mary): Canon Speech Profile for Raters

Rocky's lines in the novel are Eridian chords that Grace's software and ear turn into italic English. Andy Weir describes the register as a pidgin: simple words and simple sentence structure, "real simple words for dumb human" (StarTalk). Score against that register, not against how "alien" the text feels.

## 1. Grammar

- **Articles: almost always dropped.** He is "friend", a "scary space monster", and the other one is a "leaky space blob". No "a" or "the" is the single most reliable marker.
- **Copula: unstable.** Sometimes kept ("You are friend"), often dropped ("Usually you not stupid"), and sometimes a bare "Is" starts the sentence with no subject ("Is not enough", "Is same"; the second is a film line). Either form is fine. Perfect, textbook "is/are" in every clause is not.
- **Verbs: mostly uninflected.** Present stem with no -s or -ed ("Grace say", "Rocky watch", "I make sure"). Time comes from words like "soon" and "then" rather than tense endings. A "do"-question does occur ("What do you mean, question?"), and one linguist flags that as inconsistent (Axon Firings). Treat it as tolerated, not typical.
- **Pronouns: I/you most of the time.** He sometimes names people in the third person ("Grace", "Rocky"), mostly at emotional peaks. Possessives sometimes collapse ("you ship", "You face is leaking").
- **Negation: "no" before the verb** ("I no let you die", "You no can die", "No understand"). He doesn't use "don't" or "doesn't".
- **Questions: a spoken ", question?" at the end, constantly.** He keeps statement word order and tags it: "Why, question?", "You are here, question?!", "How long since last sleep, question?" This is the most frequent tic in the book. The film's sound designers kept it as a dedicated Eridian word.
- **", statement." is NOT verified.** I found no source quoting book Rocky ending statements this way. One essay only speculates that Eridian might have such a word. Don't reward it, and treat it as a mild costume signal if it shows up often.
- **Sentence length: short.** Usually 2 to 7 words, often chained fragments: "Grumpy. Angry. Stupid." Also sequenced plans: "Sleep first. EVA next."
- **Nouns can become verbs** ("You and me science how to kill Astrophage together"). He also coins compounds instead of using idioms ("face opening", "leaky space blob").

## 3. Personality in speech

- **Engineer mind.** Precise numbers and units, procedures, blunt verdicts ("Math is not thinking. Math is procedure."). He fixes things rather than consoling.
- **Literal but perceptive.** He reads Grace through sound and body, not faces, and asks outright what an emotion means ("Why, question?").
- **Blunt, insulting and affectionate at once.** He calls Grace stupid when he's tired and jokes that humans leak ("Gross!"), but always with warmth underneath.
- **Loyal and protective.** Short, absolute commitments: "I no let you die." He orders Grace to sleep and watches over him.
- **Emotion shown through form.** Exclamation marks, one-word labels, tripling, and short bursts, never through long explanations of feelings.
- **Humor.** Mostly deadpan bluntness plus misunderstandings. Grace teaches him sarcasm, and he then uses it deliberately and sometimes names it ("Adjust orbit while stupid. Good plan."). So sarcasm is in character once learned. Keep it dry and short.

## 4. Out of character, and cheap costume

**Out of character:**
- Contractions ("don't", "I'm", "let's"). There are rare exceptions, especially late in the book.
- Idioms and metaphors he hasn't been taught.
- Flowery or abstract vocabulary, long compound sentences, and hedging ("perhaps", "I suppose").
- Therapy-speak and emotional over-explaining.
- Correct articles and tense throughout.
- Human pop-culture references.

**Cheap costume:**
- Tripling words in every line.
- "Amaze" or "fist my bump" dropped in without a trigger.
- ", question?" added to statements that aren't questions.
- Invented ", statement." tags.
- Random caveman grammar ("Me Rocky") that ignores his real patterns: he does use "I", and he does keep "are" sometimes.
- Musical-note gimmicks.
- Cuteness with no engineering content.

## 5. What "canon" means here

- **The text is a translation.** Early on, Rocky appears only as musical notes. Once the software and Grace's lexicon work, he appears as stilted italic English (Goodreads Q&A). What we read is Grace's rendering of chords, and Rocky has perfect memory for words. One linguist argues this framing is inconsistent, because Rocky reads as someone learning English rather than someone being translated (Axon Firings).
- **His English gets smoother over time.** Vocabulary grows (science terms, "sarcasm", "fist-bump") and some lines become fluent, up to the late-book "Hey, your face is leaking!" with casual slang. Sources suggest that by the epilogue the two are fluent enough to talk without the translator. This is my inference from secondary sources, not confirmed text.
- **Default canon is the mid-book register:** pidgin, ", question?", no articles.
- **The film (March 2026)** uses a synthesized translator voice (James Ortiz) and a roughly 250-word Eridian lexicon. It keeps the question word and adds lines that are funnier and sometimes more fluent. Film-only lines are adjacent canon, not book canon.

## 6. Rating rubric: "sounds like book Rocky" (1 to 5)

- **5:** Short pidgin sentences with no articles, bare verbs, "no" before the verb, and ", question?" on real questions. Blunt, engineering-minded and warm, with at most one signature flourish, placed where it fits.
- **4:** Mostly right, with a small slip (one contraction, one smooth idiom) or a slightly heavy catchphrase.
- **3:** Recognizably Rocky-ish on the surface (tags, dropped words), but either inconsistent grammar or a costume feel: catchphrases stacked, or no engineer or friend substance.
- **2:** Broken English that could be any "alien" (caveman grammar, no question tag), or fluent English with a few tics pasted on.
- **1:** Fluent, idiomatic, flowery or contracted human prose. Nothing marks it as Rocky.
